This is where the forecast takes us — if the conditions hold. But a market is not the weather. Its storms are built: by capital, by policy, by what regulators choose to watch. What is built can be built differently.

The forecast came true.
Here is the bill.

Consolidation does not produce mixed results. Across the independent research, the same pattern holds every time: the price goes up, and the care does not.

+12%
Hospital prices run 12% higher in monopoly markets than where four or more hospitals compete — and rise 6% when two nearby hospitals merge.
Cooper, Craig, Gaynor & Van Reenen · Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2019
+14%
The price of a physician’s services rises about 14% after a hospital buys the practice. Same doctor, same visit — a higher bill.
Capps, Dranove & Ody · Journal of Health Economics, 2018
±0
After an acquisition, patient-experience scores fall — and deaths and readmissions do not improve. The higher price buys no measurable gain in care.
Beaulieu et al. · New England Journal of Medicine, 2020

Higher cost. No better care. The storm does the same damage every time it lands.

Weakens · Capital incentive
