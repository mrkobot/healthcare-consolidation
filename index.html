This is where the forecast takes us — if the conditions hold. But a market is not the weather. Its storms are built: by capital, by policy, by what regulators choose to watch. What is built can be built differently.

The forecast came true.
Here is the bill.

Consolidation does not produce mixed results. Across the independent research, the same pattern holds every time: the price goes up, and the care does not.

+12%
Hospital prices run 12% higher in monopoly markets than where four or more hospitals compete — and rise 6% when two nearby hospitals merge.
Cooper, Craig, Gaynor & Van Reenen · Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2019
+14%
The price of a physician’s services rises about 14% after a hospital buys the practice. Same doctor, same visit — a higher bill.
Capps, Dranove & Ody · Journal of Health Economics, 2018
±0
After an acquisition, patient-experience scores fall — and deaths and readmissions do not improve. The higher price buys no measurable gain in care.
Beaulieu et al. · New England Journal of Medicine, 2020

Higher cost. No better care. The storm does the same damage every time it lands.

The problem is not that healthcare is integrating. It is what we have required healthcare to become in order to integrate.

Move right only when the structure to the left cannot deliver the capability you need.

Integration can preserve competition — or quietly end it. The test is contestability.

1Can a new competitor still enter?
2Can a physician or organization leave?
3Can a payer or purchaser contract around the structure?
4Can a patient meaningfully choose around it?
5Can the arrangement be unwound without destroying the capability it created?
When the answer to these disappears, the market can lose competition even while the legal entities stay separate.

The same five forces, redesigned

Not five forces matched to five fixes — a diagnostic of how better market design changes the incentives underneath consolidation.
Fragmentation
Make independence economically viable without isolation. Shared infrastructure and networks reduce the penalty for staying independent while preserving exit and competition.
Industry shock
Build shared risk, infrastructure, liquidity support, and coordinated response that do not require an ownership transfer to absorb a disruption.
Capital liquidity
Change what earns the return. Reduce structures that reward acquisition independent of operating improvement — site-neutral payment is one concrete example.
Enforcement vacancy
Evaluate cumulative market control — serial, vertical, and below-threshold aggregation — not only individual headline transactions.
Pricing opacity
Move past disclosure toward usable comparability: standardized price, quality, and episode definitions that let purchasers actually act.
The goal is not to reduce these forces to zero. It is to reduce the extent to which they make ownership the default answer to every integration problem.
